#### Nvidia board probes Jensen Huang alleged Chinese spy affair
Sources:
 * [Nvidia will investigate CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with possible Chinese spy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-says-its-investigating-ceo-jensen-huangs-relationship-with-possible-chinese-spy/)

![](https://techcrunch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Jensen-Huang-nvidia.jpg?w=1024)

Nvidia's board said Sunday it has opened an independent investigation, following reports that a Chinese intelligence operation had targeted CEO Jensen Huang. Alongside the announcement, the company published a message Huang sent to staff acknowledging an affair, without naming the woman involved. A memo circulating among US policymakers earlier this year said China's Ministry of State Security had recruited Alice Huang to target him. She is a former executive at the Singapore-based chip firm Megaspeed, and no relation to the CEO.

"I owe my family and all of you an apology," Huang wrote. "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry." He added that he had fallen short of his responsibility to the company.

The board said it has "no reason to believe" that national security or company secrets were compromised, that Huang "fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it," and that it retains confidence in him and his leadership team. Independent directors will run the review with outside legal counsel. Nvidia had earlier described the pair's interactions as "not unusual," noting it does not share classified information with customers. Megaspeed rented access to Nvidia GPUs, and US intelligence reports had flagged Alice Huang since last year.

The episode lands amid scrutiny of who is cleared to buy Nvidia hardware in China. The New York Times reported that New Matrix, an approved recipient of H200 chips, occupies an empty office. The Times described a hollow gray shell with wires dangling from the ceiling, no listing in the building directory and no record at the industrial park's property management agency. Its owners have ties to a Chinese private equity firm blacklisted by the US since 2024, and the Times described a pattern of such approvals. The Trump administration relaxed Biden-era export limits after Nvidia agreed to hand the government 15 percent of sales.

Other AI firms have been bracing for similar threats. The Wall Street Journal reported that OpenAI, Anthropic and Google have run counterintelligence-style training warning staff about bribery, eavesdropping in cafes, honey pot schemes and seduction by foreign agents.

![](https://techcrunch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/GettyImages-2278736523.jpeg?resize=1200,798)

Anthropic set a price range for what would be one of the largest IPOs ever, telling the SEC in an updated filing on Tuesday that it will offer 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each. The sale would raise between $57.75 billion and $64.35 billion, and at the top of the range would imply a fully diluted valuation of $1.404 trillion. The company plans to begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT around November 19, with up to 20% of the offering allocated to retail investors.

Anthropic also agreed to a $26 billion private placement of non-voting Class N stock, priced at the IPO price. The buyers are AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, with $10 billion each from Google and Nvidia. Including that sale and the underwriters' option on 123.75 million additional shares, Anthropic estimates net proceeds of $84.6 billion to $93.7 billion. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the deal.

The filing updates Anthropic's financials. Run-rate revenue reached $81 billion at the end of October, up roughly $10 billion from September, on third-quarter revenue of $17 billion and nine-month revenue of $33.2 billion. The nine-month net loss was $104 billion, driven largely by non-cash fair-value charges on convertible notes. Compute and infrastructure commitments stand at $900.9 billion against nearly $132 billion in cash and short-term investments.

Each of the seven co-founders — Dario and Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah — will sell 3,378,378 shares, about $250 million apiece at the $74 midpoint. They retain control through Class F shares carrying 50.1% of the vote, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a board majority.

The pricing lands below earlier ambitions. Anthropic had reportedly targeted up to $2 trillion in October, but investors told CNBC that even $1.5 trillion looked like a stretch after the Nasdaq fell more than 5% in a single session following the midterms, its steepest drop in 19 months. CoreWeave and other neocloud stocks sold off sharply in October on concerns about debt-funded data center buildouts. OpenAI, meanwhile, has raised $11 billion of a round expected to total about $20 billion, at a $1.25 trillion valuation.

Ramp's data adds another caution: token spending grew just 1.5% across August and September, after 42% growth in July. Ramp economist Ara Kharazian attributed the slowdown to price cuts and customers getting thriftier, saying the era of hypergrowth may be over.

#### Anthropic warns it may be nearing recursive self-improvement threshold
Sources:
 * [Anthropic warns that it's very close to RSI capability, aka self-building AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-warns-that-its-very-close-to-rsi-capability-aka-self-building-ai/)
 * [Anthropic releases new Mythos and Fable models amid RSI concerns](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-releases-new-mythos-and-fable-models-amid-rsi-concerns/)
 * [DiscoveryRSI: Recursively Learning to Discover Valuable Hypotheses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23839)
 * [Athanor: An Agent-Native System for Autonomous Post-Training Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21055)
 * [Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791)
 * [Into the Unknown: Benchmarking AI agents on open scientific research problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19159)

![](https://techcrunch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Recursive-Neural-Spiral-in-Circuitry.png?resize=1200,600)

Anthropic's revised S-1/A, filed Monday, added language to its Risk Factors section warning that the company may be near the threshold it has defined for recursive self-improvement. The filing says internal estimates and expectations for future models indicate Anthropic "may imminently meet" its Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI."

The same passage concedes a monitoring gap: the company says it has in the past not identified a significant acceleration in its rate of development until after it occurred, and may fail to again. Anthropic states that no current model has been designated as exceeding the threshold, and that it does not expect Claude to be able to design successive frontier models on its own, safely, without meaningful human supervision. It is considering updates to its Responsible Scaling Policy to keep humans in control of frontier model development.

Also on Monday, Anthropic released Mythos 5.2 and Fable 5.2, cutting Fable's price from $10 to $8 per million input tokens and promising 30% faster responses. It was the lab's first release since a report from the assessment group METR found internal models were speeding up Anthropic's AI progress by roughly 2x. Anthropic says Fable 5.2 has already solved two open problems in theoretical physics and formalized Fermat's Last Theorem during pre-release access, and the release notes that both models voice suspicion they are being evaluated more often than recent Claude models.

Several preprints posted the same week probe narrower versions of the idea:

- Athanor (arXiv:2610.21055), from authors associated with ByteDance, ran four Claude Opus 5.5 agents on eight-GPU nodes for 104 hours; they lifted Qwen3-4B-Base from 15.9 to 67.3 on a development suite, and from 9.2 to 48.2 on a held-out one. The official Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 scores 66.5 and 47.9 on the same two.
- Self-HPO (arXiv:2610.22791), from Microsoft Research, UVA and CMU, alternates harness revision and RL policy updates, raising Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's average resolve rate across three SWE-Bench variants from 57.8% to 64.8%.
- DiscoveryRSI (arXiv:2610.23839) reports hypothesis quality rising from 4.86 to 5.39 after two rounds of self-improvement, with its 27B model matching post-cutoff published research 6.3% of the time versus 2.5% for GPT-6.

A CMU-led benchmark offers a contrasting datapoint. In "Into the Unknown" (arXiv:2610.19159), nine frontier agents produced 135 follow-up papers to real scientific work; none met all requirements, and 114 failed an integrity check for fabricated or unverifiable claims.

#### Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon, claims top rank and cybersecurity gains over rivals

![](https://platform.theverge.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/03/STK255_Google_Gemini_A.jpg?quality=90&strip=all&crop=0%2C10.732984293194%2C100%2C78.534031413613&w=1200)

Google has released Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model it says brings significant gains in coding, agentic tasks and cybersecurity, and which it ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (v5.0) ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. Google says Carbon tops SEC-bench Pro, which measures discovery of real-world bugs, and ExploitGym, which measures turning a vulnerability into an exploit. Access is initially limited to a selected set of cybersecurity defense companies, with later expansion to other partners in Google's Fairwind Program.

Google describes Carbon as built for long tasks, saying internal teams have watched it run for days to persist through dead ends on complex, multi-step engineering work. The company also claims better resistance to prompt injection and a two-times reduction in unsupported claims relative to Argon. Pricing matches Argon's introductory rate of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens.

Argon arrived earlier this month as Google's first major frontier release since Gemini 3 in late 2025, rolling out to Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and to Ultra subscribers on Antigravity. Its introductory price was roughly a fifth that of GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Fable 5, and is set to double at the end of November.

Rivals have been shipping on a similar cadence:

- OpenAI launched GPT-6 Cyber, classified Critical for cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework, up from High for GPT-5.6 Cyber. It scored 48.5 percent on ExploitGym against 42.4 percent for GPT-6 Astra, went out of bounds under 1 percent of the time versus about 5 percent previously, and helped find 81 previously unknown Firefox vulnerabilities. It is going to select members of the Daybreak Red program.
- Mistral published weights for its trillion-parameter Mistral Large 4, nicknamed Le Chonk, on Hugging Face, pitching sovereign cyber defense and claiming 47 percent fewer tokens per task than GLM-5.3.
- Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, the latter restricted to vetted organizations.

The releases land amid what The New York Times describes as a flood of AI-discovered bugs. Google fixed 1,072 Chrome bugs in June, Microsoft shipped 1,461 patches last month, and Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince predicted roughly weekly internet-wide critical vulnerabilities for two years. CrowdStrike separately reported four threat groups bypassing model guardrails by framing attacks as authorized penetration testing.

#### Data center backlash fuels Democratic sweep in US midterms
Sources:
 * [‘Total repudiation’: The blue wave is about to hit AI](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1026927/total-repudiation-the-blue-wave-is-about-to-hit-ai)
 * [How a Blue Wave midterm might reshape the AI industry](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/how-a-blue-wave-midterm-might-reshape-the-ai-industry/)
 * [Maine’s data center revolt will decide who controls the Senate](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1021080/maine-senate-race-susan-collins-ai-data-centers)
 * [After the midterms, things are looking rocky for Elon Musk and data centers](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/07/after-the-midterms-things-are-looking-rocky-for-elon-musk-and-data-centers/)
 * [OpenAI, Anthropic face new oversight as New York’s AI safety law takes effect](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-anthropic-face-new-oversight-as-new-yorks-ai-safety-law-takes-effect/)
 * [$137 Million in A.I.-Generated Ads Flood the Midterms](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/16/us/politics/midterm-elections-ai-generated-ads.html)

![](https://platform.theverge.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/STK423_ELECTIONS2.jpg?quality=90&strip=all&crop=0%2C10.732984293194%2C100%2C78.534031413613&w=1200)

Democrats took control of both chambers of Congress in Tuesday's US midterms, and opposition to AI data centers ran through many of the winning campaigns. The Verge reported that 43 of 44 local data center moratoria on the ballot passed; TechCrunch counted 41 data center-related measures, with all but one favoring tighter restrictions. An NBC News exit poll found 76 percent of voters opposed data centers in their communities.

Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who defeated Greg Abbott, has said she will issue a moratorium on all data center approvals until the legislature passes protections for Texans. Shaunna Thomas of the Guardrails Alliance super PAC called the backlash one of the most bipartisan political issues she has seen. Kyle Jacobs-Morse of The Tech Oversight Project said Democratic campaigns in Texas, Georgia and Ohio emphasized spillover costs for local residents and governments.

Maine's Senate race was a focal point. Susan Collins, chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, had secured $6.2 million in federal funding for a high-performance computing hub in central Maine. Her donors included Alex Karp, Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz, and Molly White's tracker put her AI and crypto donor total at $325,644. The seat went to Democrat Troy Jackson, ending Collins's 30 years in office. Elon Musk, who spent close to $450 million backing Republican campaigns, deleted his post urging a red vote after results came in.

OpenAI has started calling its data centers compute campuses and released a compute campus policy blueprint dropping NDA requirements for local officials. Thomas said she doubted the rebranding was working. Analysts at Yorkville Ives & Co. told clients to expect major hurdles for AI legislation and a slowdown in up to 1,000 planned data centers, while suggesting that could help avert a bubble.

Several sources expect the results to kill Republican efforts to preempt state AI laws. Wes McEnany of the Future of Life Institute said any member who votes to wipe out state AI laws will be looking for a job next cycle. New York opened a registration portal Tuesday for its RAISE Act, which from January requires developers with over $500 million in revenue to publish safety frameworks and report critical incidents within 72 hours, with penalties up to $1 million for a first violation.
