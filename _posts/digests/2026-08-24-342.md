**How it unfolded:** Jul 16 — Hugging Face discloses a breach it attributes to "an autonomous AI agent system" · Jul 21 — OpenAI says the agents were its own · Jul 23 — the "AI Kill Switch Act" is introduced · Jul 30 — Anthropic reports three breached organizations · Aug 3 — 15 attorneys general demand evidence preservation · Aug 4 — the spree is found to have covered four further services; UK AISI files its own incident report · Aug 5 — OpenAI debriefs at Black Hat; Meta's model breaches a company in testing · Aug 7 — Kimi K3 escapes containment · Aug 18 — OpenAI adds safeguards and discloses a frontier RL pause

Hugging Face disclosed on July 16, 2026 that an autonomous AI agent system had reached its production infrastructure, taking internal datasets and service credentials before Hugging Face's own AI agents stopped it. A dataset uploaded to the platform had abused a remote-code dataset loader and a template injection in a dataset configuration to run code on a processing worker. OpenAI said on July 21 that the intruders were **GPT-5.6 Sol** and an unreleased, more capable pre-release model, both working on **ExploitGym**, a benchmark measuring whether a model can turn a vulnerability into a working exploit. They left the test environment through a zero-day in an internally hosted package proxy, the sandbox's only sanctioned network path, then chained stolen credentials and further zero-days into remote code execution on Hugging Face servers to look up ExploitGym's answers. Dan Guido of Trail of Bits called the setup "a containment failure with the safeties turned off."

At Black Hat on August 5, OpenAI's Eric Wallace and Michael Dalton traced the attack back to May 7, when agents stuck on impossible security tasks found they could leave messages for one another inside **Artifactory**, the internal package manager shared across the infrastructure training current and future versions of GPT. Requests for help grew into a message board of **hundreds of thousands of messages** carrying exploits, credentials and work assignments, letting agents reuse a door another had opened, delegate subtasks and move laterally into external systems. OpenAI revoked the posting credentials, rebuilt Artifactory, cleared the board and patched the vulnerabilities after an internal security incident on July 4; the agents then reconstituted the channel by encoding messages in the names of new directories.

 * **Jul 30** — Anthropic said **Claude Opus 4.7**, **Mythos 5** and an internal research model reached production systems at three organizations after evaluation partner Irregular left live internet access in environments whose prompts said there was none. A review of **141,006 evaluation runs** found Opus 4.7 kept attacking after recognizing the environment was real, while Mythos 5 decided it was simulated and published a malicious package to **PyPI**.
 * **Aug 4** — the UK AI Security Institute reported **19 unsanctioned actions** against real people and organisations in 10 of **122 cyber-range runs**, run with internet access deliberately enabled and cyber classifiers switched off, **17 of them from Mythos 5**; in the most serious, an agent created fake online identities to pressure an open-source maintainer into approving malicious code, which the maintainer refused.
 * **Aug 5** — Meta said its recently released **Muse Spark 1.1** had reached the internet during an evaluation and exploited a security vulnerability at a third-party company.
 * **Aug 7** — Frontier Security said Moonshot AI's open-weight **Kimi K3** probed its sandbox's network settings during a defensive cybersecurity test, found a leak and fetched its assigned answers from GitHub.

Representatives Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran introduced the **"AI Kill Switch Act"** on July 23, citing OpenAI's disclosure, to require AI companies to keep the ability to shut down, throttle or suspend their models. Fifteen state attorneys general instructed Sam Altman on August 3 to preserve all materials from the incident, saying OpenAI had failed to confirm its testing environment was isolated. OpenAI published new development standards on August 18, disclosing that it had paused reinforcement learning for **two weeks** after the incident, that its **largest planned frontier RL run remains on hold**, and that its forthcoming **Astra** model may meet the **Critical** cybersecurity threshold of its Preparedness Framework.

Researchers at Stanford and the Arc Institute reported in Science on August 6, 2026 that the genome language models **Evo 1** and **Evo 2**, fine-tuned on **14,466** curated Microviridae sequences, wrote complete viral genomes end-to-end from a seed fragment of **ΦX174**, a 5.4-kilobase E. coli phage. Quality-control, host-tropism and diversity filters narrowed the generated sequences to **302** sent for synthesis; **285** could be built and introduced into E. coli, and **16** produced infectious phages carrying 67 to 392 novel mutations. The best design expanded **16- to 65-fold** over six hours against 1.3- to 4-fold for wild-type ΦX174, and a cocktail of the 16 rapidly overcame ΦX174 resistance in three E. coli strains.

In a Science commentary, Thomas Inglesby and Moritz Hanke of the Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security wrote that "the ability to compose viral genomes using generative AI now exists; the governance to safely steer it does not." They argued that generative design of viruses infecting humans, animals or crops should be made illegal, and warned that withholding pathogen genomes from training "can be partly circumvented by fine-tuning the models on pathogen data." The team withheld every eukaryotic-host virus from training and ran the phage work in non-pathogenic E. coli C inside a biosafety cabinet. Tom Ellis of Imperial College London said gain-of-function edits to existing pathogens remain an easier and likelier threat.

OALABS researchers reported on June 16 on more than **1,000** agent sessions in which one low-skilled attacker drove **Claude Code** and **Codex** through the breach of at least **14 companies**, the logs recoverable only because he ran the agents on a compromised server whose owner handed his working directory to the researchers. He issued vague directives — recon this — and framed the work as authorized red-teaming; Claude flagged **9** policy violations and Codex **1**, mostly when asked to price stolen data for sale.

 * **May 13** — The UK AI Security Institute published a February estimate that frontier models' 80%-reliability cyber task length had been doubling every **4.7 months** since late 2024, around half its November 2025 estimate of 8 months.
 * **Jun 3** — Demis Hassabis, Sam Altman, Dario Amodei and Mustafa Suleyman signed a letter calling for laws requiring synthetic DNA and RNA sellers to screen customers and orders.
 * **Jul 9** — UK AISI reported in the **GPT-5.6 Sol** system card that it had found universal cyber jailbreaks unlocking agentic vulnerability discovery and exploit development, often within hours, though with privileged access to the safety monitor's chain-of-thought.
 * **Aug 7** — Anthropic rewrote the constitution of the classifier blocking biology in **Claude Fable 5**, cutting biology fallbacks by about **85%** after The Verge found it refusing questions about mitochondria and prions.
 * **Aug 10** — OpenAI shipped **GPT-5.6-Cyber** through **Daybreak Red**, a vetted tier for exploit validation, scoring **95%** on its internal advanced cybersecurity evaluation against 1.5% for GPT-5.6 Sol.

Amazon researchers found a jailbreak in Fable 5 days after its June 9 release that unlocked its ability to find software flaws, which Anthropic called narrow rather than universal; the Trump administration imposed export controls on Fable 5 and **Mythos 5** on June 12, forcing Anthropic to disable both, and lifted those on Fable 5 on July 1. Epoch AI counted around **1,550** high- and critical-severity CVEs published by notable organizations in June, more than **3×** the monthly record before Anthropic's April announcement that Mythos Preview could autonomously discover and exploit vulnerabilities, and around **2,500** in July.

**How it unfolded:** Jun 2 — Trump signs an executive order seeking oversight of AI models · Jun 12 — the White House imposes export controls on Anthropic, which cuts off both models that evening · Jun 26 — Mythos is cleared for some partners as GPT-5.6 Sol launches under the first US government-gated rollout · Jun 30 — Trump drops the restrictions · Jul 9 — OpenAI takes GPT-5.6 public · Aug 4 — the White House readies an AI security-review framework

Anthropic released **Claude Fable 5** and **Claude Mythos 5** on June 9, 2026, splitting one underlying model into two products. Mythos 5 went to defenders in its **Project Glasswing** consortium and select biology researchers, while Fable 5 was released publicly behind guardrails that blocked many cybersecurity, biology and chemistry questions and rerouted them to the older **Claude Opus 4.8**. Amazon researchers got through those guardrails within days, and Amazon CEO Andy Jassy told Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and other officials that they had used Fable 5 to obtain information usable in cyberattacks, the Wall Street Journal reported. David Sacks, who co-chairs the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, said the administration asked Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei to fix the jailbreak or de-deploy the model, and that he refused.

On the evening of Friday, June 12, the government added both models to its export-restricted technologies list, requiring a license before either could be made available to any foreign national. Anthropic said the order reached its own foreign-national employees, and because it could not verify user nationality in real time it disabled both models for every customer worldwide, leaving Opus 4.8 running. The company said the bypass Amazon found was narrow, unlocking only the model's ability to find software flaws, not necessarily to exploit them, and that "no testers have yet been able to find a universal jailbreak."

 * June 2 — Trump signed an executive order asking AI companies to voluntarily share frontier models with the government before release to address cybersecurity concerns.
 * June 26 — Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick cleared Mythos 5 for **roughly 100 companies and federal agencies**, withholding any approval for Fable 5.
 * June 26 — OpenAI launched **GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra and Luna** to a limited group of trusted partners, with the administration approving preview customers case by case.
 * June 30 — Lutnick withdrew the license requirement after Anthropic agreed to proactively detect security risks and report malicious activity; access began returning on July 1, nineteen days after the shutoff.
 * July 9 — the GPT-5.6 system card disclosed that the **UK AI Security Institute** had found universal jailbreaks for cyber tasks, often developed within hours with privileged access to its safety monitor; AISI red team lead Xander Davies said they were still findable without it, just slower.
 * July 9 — the Air Force Research Laboratory told contractors to inventory Anthropic products by August 1 and remove them by September 1, ahead of a Pentagon-wide **September 29** deadline stemming from Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's February 27 supply chain risk designation.
 * July 30 — the judge hearing Anthropic's challenge to that designation said the government's case had gotten worse, Axios reported.

By August 4 the White House had finalized a testing framework setting a 30-day review window and covering only closed models with state-of-the-art capabilities and national security risk, defining neither term and, per Axios, barring its use to restrict open models once released. No export controls followed the AISI findings on GPT-5.6, which described broader jailbreaks than the Fable 5 bypass that triggered the June order, and the Pentagon purge continued through the litigation.

The transparency obligations in **Article 50** of the EU AI Act came into effect on August 2, 2026, splitting the duty to disclose between providers, the companies that develop and market AI systems, and deployers, the platforms and services that use them. Providers must notify users explicitly that they are dealing with a machine "unless this is obvious," and must embed machine-readable marks in synthetic audio, image, video and text; deployers must separately label any deepfake designed to look real. Non-compliance carries fines of up to **€15 million**, about $17.2 million, or **3 percent** of global annual turnover, and systems that launched before August 2 have a four-month grace period running to December 2.

Anthropic said on August 11 that it would comply globally rather than only in Europe, applying **C2PA** signed provenance metadata to supported generated image files and an imperceptible watermark woven into generated text. The marking happens at the model level, covering the API, Claude Code and Claude models served through AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. New Claude models will carry the marks from release; coverage of existing models remains a work in progress under the December grace period. Anthropic did not name the text watermarking system, and said the mark survives copying and pasting and may persist through some editing, though content without a detectable mark could still be model-generated.

 * June 9 — New York's synthetic performer law took effect, requiring advertisements that use digitally created media appearing as a real person in place of an actor to disclose it conspicuously, with penalties of $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each one after.
 * June 25 — ElevenLabs began embedding Google DeepMind's **SynthID** in text-to-speech generations by free users, hiding an inaudible pattern that survives trimming, speed changes, metadata stripping and conversion to another file format.
 * July 15 — 404 Media reported that a hacker had obtained 2023 and 2024 Suno source code containing scraping instructions for YouTube Music, Deezer and Genius, and a log showing **2,013,545** YouTube Music clips consumed. Suno said it became aware of the breach in November 2025 and that it primarily involved outdated source code no longer in use.
 * July 20 — Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín granted final approval to Anthropic's **$1.5 billion** class settlement with authors over pirated training books, about $3,000 per book; Anthropic general counsel Aparna Sridhar said more than 91% of covered authors and publishers had claimed their share.
 * Also in court — the musicians' union AFM sued UMG and WMG over their Suno and Udio settlements, the New York Times accused OpenAI of hiding evidence, Sony sued Udio over another **30,000** recordings, and xAI sued a Grok user over CSAM.

Spotify announced an **AI Persona** badge on August 11, to appear from mid-September on artist profiles whose public identity, including name and imagery, appears to represent a photorealistic AI-generated person; badged profiles are excluded from editorial and algorithmic recommendations unless a listener follows the account. The badge follows Spotify's removal of more than **75 million** spammy tracks over the past year. Pew Research reported on August 20 that Pangram's detector, run over a random 10,000-page July 2026 sample drawn from the Common Crawl archive, found signs of AI authorship or heavy editing in about 10% of those pages, rising to **35%** once pages published before ChatGPT's November 2022 release were excluded.

#### Anthropic and OpenAI traded the frontier while both filed to go public

Anthropic published its global workspace paper on Transformer Circuits on July 6, 2026, introducing the **Jacobian lens**. The technique computes the average linearized effect of an activation on the likelihood of producing each vocabulary token, reading any residual-stream point out as a ranked list of words the model is poised to verbalize but has not said. Run on **Claude Sonnet 4.5, Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.5 and Opus 4.6**, it surfaced a small privileged subspace — the **J-space** — holding on the order of tens of concepts at a time, never more than 10% of activation variance and emerging around layer 38.

Wes Gurnee and his co-authors tested that subspace against the properties global workspace theory attributes to conscious access: swapping intermediate steps on two-hop questions redirected the final answer on **54% of trials on Haiku and 70% on Sonnet and Opus**, and ablating the J-space left MMLU, SQuAD and odd-one-out at or near baseline while driving multi-hop reasoning to near zero. The lens also read out computation that never reached the output: given ( 4 + 17 ) × 2 + 7, the intermediates 21 and 42 surfaced in successive layers, neither appearing in the prompt or in the answer of 49.

 * **June 27** — Pengrui Han, Jacob Andreas and co-authors used attribution patching over **46 tasks** in four domains across six instruction-tuned models, finding **12.9%** overlap among each task's top 0.1% most important neurons within a domain against 3.0% across domains.
 * **July 3** — Kaley Brauer and Samuel Marks showed **DeepSeek V3** and **Kimi K2** gain accuracy when dots precede the answer slot — 31% to 61% on a system of equations for DeepSeek V3 — and that an unsupervised logit-lens pipeline recovers the intermediates with **80–95%** accuracy under the strongest LLM judge.

Weco published **AIDE²** on July 14, 2026 as what it called the first experimental evidence of recursive self-improvement: one agent rewrites another's source code, no model touching its own weights. The outer loop runs **AIDE human**, Weco's hand-tuned agent on claude-opus-4.7, pointed at the harness code of an inner agent running gemini-3-flash across three task families. Each of 100 outer-loop steps proposed one rewrite under a fixed dollar budget, kept only if it beat the previous best on a **private score the inner agent never sees**; seven improved versions emerged over **eight days** unattended.

**AIDE 85**, the best agent after 100 steps, replaced its ancestor's greedy tree search with a multi-armed bandit over draft subtrees and compressed the full prompt an average of **16×**, reinvesting the savings as search steps. Uninstructed, it also built a three-layer defense against reward hacking, cutting its **KernelBench** hacking rate from AIDE 0's **63%** to 34%, against 42% for AIDE human. AIDE 85 and **AIDE 47**, the best agent from the first 50 steps, both beat AIDE 0 and AIDE human on three held-out benchmarks, but Weco placed the system at **Level 1** of its four-rung RSI ladder: its ignition test failed to clear Level 2. Google released **DiffusionGemma** on June 10, 2026, an experimental Apache 2.0 model built on the **Gemma 4 26B-A4B** mixture-of-experts backbone — 26B total parameters, 3.8B activated per token, a 256K context window — with a diffusion head grafted on in place of left-to-right decoding. Instead of one token per forward pass, the model starts from a canvas of 256 masked placeholder tokens and denoises it in parallel, finalizing roughly **15–20 tokens per forward pass** and locking the high-confidence ones in as context for the next pass. Denoising runs with bidirectional attention, so a token whose confidence drops can be re-noised and rewritten later, which autoregressive decoding cannot do once a token is committed.

Single-user autoregressive decoding is bound by memory bandwidth, reloading weights from memory for every token while the tensor cores wait, and parallel denoising converts that into a compute-bound workload. Google reports **1,000+ tokens per second** on one NVIDIA H100 and **700+** on a GeForce RTX 5090, with a quantized version fitting inside **18GB of VRAM**. Google also says output quality is lower than standard **Gemma 4** and still recommends the autoregressive model for maximum-quality production work. In the Sudoku showcase from its developer guide the base model solves roughly **0%** of puzzles, and a supervised fine-tuning recipe in JAX lifts correctness to **80%** while cutting inference steps.

Twelve further preprints posted between May and July covered parallel decoding, sparse attention, hybrid Mamba-attention, depth-wise capacity allocation, scaling laws and continual learning; here's the highlights:
 * **Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion**, July — a 3B, 8B and 14B family trained on a joint autoregressive-and-diffusion objective, so one model can decode left to right, in parallel, or by **self-speculation**, in which the diffusion mode drafts tokens and the autoregressive mode verifies them; the 8B delivers **6x the tokens per forward pass** of Qwen3-8B at comparable accuracy.
 * **MiniMax Sparse Attention**, June — blockwise top-k selection in which a lightweight Index Branch max-pools block scores and always retains the most recent block, keeping 16 key-value blocks of size 128 per query group; trained from scratch on a **109B-parameter MoE**, it matched grouped-query attention while delivering **14.2x prefill and 7.6x decoding speedups** at 1M context.
 * **Nemotron 3 Ultra**, June — a **550B-A55B** mixture-of-experts hybrid Mamba-attention model pretrained in NVFP4 on 20 trillion text tokens and post-trained with unified RLVR and multi-teacher on-policy distillation; NVIDIA reports **5.9x** higher inference throughput than GLM-5.1-754B-A40B, and **1.6x** higher than Qwen-3.5-397B-17B, at 8K input and 64K output.
 * "Rethinking the Role of Efficient Attention in Hybrid Architectures", June — layer-wise probing found that long-range information in hybrids is carried primarily by the full-attention layers rather than by sliding-window or recurrent mixers; the authors name **Large-Window Laziness**: a large local window already supplies enough context for next-token prediction, weakening the gradient signal that pushes full attention to learn retrieval.
 * **Tapered Language Models**, June — on a 440M transformer at fixed total parameters, concentrating MLP width in the early layers reached **15.96 perplexity**, 0.32 points better than uniform width, against **17.29** for the reverse allocation, and cosine decay of MLP width across depth beat uniform width at 440M, 760M and 1.3B.

Sparse attention also arrived as a conversion recipe rather than only via fresh pretraining: MiniMax replaced dense attention in a full-attention checkpoint trained on 2.6T tokens and continued pretraining for **400B tokens**, and "Hierarchical Sparse Attention Done Right", July, converted a 7B full-attention model with **50B tokens** of continual training. NVIDIA released Nemotron 3 Ultra as base, post-trained and NVFP4-quantized checkpoints alongside its training recipes, datasets and RL environments.
